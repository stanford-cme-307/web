Formulate the dual of each linear program. Multipliers below are named in the order of the displayed constraints.

1. Three constraint senses

maximize4x1+2x2subject tox1+x2≤5,−2x1+x2≥1,3x1−x2=2,x1,x2≥0. \begin{aligned} \text{maximize}\quad &4x_1+2x_2\\ \text{subject to}\quad &x_1+x_2\le5,\\ &-2x_1+x_2\ge1,\\ &3x_1-x_2=2,\qquad x_1,x_2\ge0. \end{aligned}

Solution to exercise 1

Minimize 5u+v+2w5u+v+2w subject to u−2v+3w≥4u-2v+3w\ge4 and u+v−w≥2u+v-w\ge2, with u≥0u\ge0, v≤0v\le0, and ww free. In this maximization primal, the first two multipliers make the corresponding rows upper bounds when combined. The equality multiplier is free.

2. A free primal variable

minimize3x1+x2+2x3subject tox1+4x2+2x3≥2,2x1−x2+3x3≤3,x1−x2+2x3=1,x1,x3≥0,x2 free. \begin{aligned} \text{minimize}\quad &3x_1+x_2+2x_3\\ \text{subject to}\quad &x_1+4x_2+2x_3\ge2,\\ &2x_1-x_2+3x_3\le3,\\ &x_1-x_2+2x_3=1,\qquad x_1,x_3\ge0,\quad x_2\text{ free}. \end{aligned}

Solution to exercise 2

Maximize 2u+3v+w2u+3v+w subject to u+2v+w≤3,4u−v−w=1,2u+3v+2w≤2,u+2v+w\le3,\qquad 4u-v-w=1,\qquad 2u+3v+2w\le2, with u≥0u\ge0, v≤0v\le0, and ww free. The column of the free variable x2x_2 gives an equality.

3. Upper bounds on variables

maximize2x1+5x2subject to3x1+x2≤8,x1+4x2≤9,0≤x1≤2,0≤x2≤3. \begin{aligned} \text{maximize}\quad &2x_1+5x_2\\ \text{subject to}\quad &3x_1+x_2\le8,\qquad x_1+4x_2\le9,\\ &0\le x_1\le 2,\qquad 0\le x_2\le3. \end{aligned}

Solution to exercise 3

Treat x1≤2x_1\le2 and x2≤3x_2\le3 as additional rows. Minimize 8u+9v+2w+3z8u+9v+2w+3z subject to 3u+v+w≥23u+v+w\ge2 and u+4v+z≥5u+4v+z\ge5, with u,v,w,z≥0u,v,w,z\ge0.

4. A nonpositive primal variable

maximize−x1+3x2subject to−x1+x2≤2,2x1+x2=1,x1≤0,x2≥0. \begin{aligned} \text{maximize}\quad &-x_1+3x_2\\ \text{subject to}\quad &-x_1+x_2\le2,\qquad 2x_1+x_2=1,\\ &x_1\le0,\qquad x_2\ge0. \end{aligned}

Solution to exercise 4

Minimize 2u+v2u+v subject to −u+2v≤−1-u+2v\le-1 and u+v≥3u+v\ge3, with u≥0u\ge0 and vv free. Since x1≤0x_1\le0, the first column inequality has the opposite direction from the second.

5. Matrix form

Maximize c⊺xc^\intercal x subject to Ax≤bAx\le b and x≥0x\ge0, where A=[1−12031],b=(4,5),c=(1,0,2). A=\begin{bmatrix}1&-1&2\\0&3&1\end{bmatrix},\qquad b=(4,5),\qquad c=(1,0,2).

Solution to exercise 5

The dual is min⁡{b⊺y:A⊺y≥c,y≥0}\min\{b^\intercal y:A^\intercal y\ge c,\ y\ge0\}. With y=(u,v)y=(u,v), minimize 4u+5v4u+5v subject to u≥1u\ge1, −u+3v≥0-u+3v\ge0, 2u+v≥22u+v\ge2, and u,v≥0u,v\ge0.

6. Three variable sign conventions

minimize5x1−x2+2x3subject to−x1+2x2+x3≥3,4x1+x2−2x3≥1,x1≥0,x2≤0,x3 free. \begin{aligned} \text{minimize}\quad &5x_1-x_2+2x_3\\ \text{subject to}\quad &-x_1+2x_2+x_3\ge3,\qquad 4x_1+x_2-2x_3\ge1,\\ &x_1\ge0,\qquad x_2\le0,\qquad x_3\text{ free}. \end{aligned}

Solution to exercise 6

Maximize 3u+v3u+v subject to −u+4v≤5-u+4v\le5, 2u+v≥−12u+v\ge-1, and u−2v=2u-2v=2, with u,v≥0u,v\ge0. The three column relations follow from the nonnegative, nonpositive, and free primal variables, respectively.

7. A feasibility problem

minimize0subject to2x1−x2≥1,−x1+3x2≥2,x1,x2≥0. \begin{aligned} \text{minimize}\quad &0\\ \text{subject to}\quad &2x_1-x_2\ge1,\qquad -x_1+3x_2\ge2,\qquad x_1,x_2\ge0. \end{aligned}

Solution to exercise 7

Maximize u+2vu+2v subject to 2u−v≤02u-v\le0 and −u+3v≤0-u+3v\le0, with u,v≥0u,v\ge0. In fact, the only feasible dual point is (0,0)(0,0), because v≥2uv\ge 2u and u≥3vu\ge 3v.

8. Equality constraints and variable signs

maximizex1−2x2+x3subject tox1+x2+x3=4,2x1−x2+3x3=1,x1≥0,x2 free,x3≤0. \begin{aligned} \text{maximize}\quad &x_1-2x_2+x_3\\ \text{subject to}\quad &x_1+x_2+x_3=4,\qquad 2x_1-x_2+3x_3=1,\\ &x_1\ge0,\qquad x_2\text{ free},\qquad x_3\le0. \end{aligned}

Solution to exercise 8

Minimize 4u+v4u+v subject to u+2v≥1u+2v\ge1, u−v=−2u-v=-2, and u+3v≤1u+3v\le1. The two equality rows make uu and vv free.
