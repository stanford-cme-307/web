1. Slack variables and free variables

Rewrite min⁡{−2x1+x2}\min\{-2x_1+x_2\} subject to 2x1+3x2≤52x_1+3x_2\le 5, x1∈ℝx_1\in\mathbb R, and x2≥0x_2\ge 0 in standard form. Introduce a slack variable, split the free variable, and specify the decision vector and data.

Solution to exercise 1

Set x1=x1+−x1−x_1=x_1^+-x_1^- and use slack s≥0s\ge 0. For y=(x1+,x1−,x2,s)≥0y=(x_1^+,x_1^-,x_2,s)\ge 0, take A=[2−231],b=5,c=(−2,2,1,0). A=\begin{bmatrix}2&-2&3&1\end{bmatrix},\qquad b=5,\qquad c=(-2,2,1,0). The equality is 2x1+−2x1−+3x2+s=52x_1^+-2x_1^-+3x_2+s=5.

5. Enumerating bases

For A=[101011]A=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&1\\0&1&1\end{bmatrix}, b=(1,1)b=(1,1), and x≥0x\ge 0, list every basis S⊆{1,2,3}S\subseteq\{1,2,3\} that gives a BFS, and give the corresponding xx.

Solution to exercise 5

All three pairs of columns are linearly independent. The pair {1,2}\{1,2\} gives x=(1,1,0)x=(1,1,0). Each of {1,3}\{1,3\} and {2,3}\{2,3\} gives x=(0,0,1)x=(0,0,1). There are three feasible bases and two distinct basic feasible points.

6. Exposing a basic feasible point

Let xx be a BFS of F={z:Az=b,z≥0}F=\{z:Az=b,\ z\ge 0\} with basis SS. Construct cc so that xx is the unique minimizer of c⊺zc^\intercal z over FF, and use that fact to show xx is a vertex.

Solution to exercise 6

Set cj=0c_j=0 for j∈Sj\in S and cj=1c_j=1 otherwise. Every feasible cost is nonnegative, and xx has cost zero. If xx were a strict convex combination of distinct feasible points, both would have cost zero by linearity, contradicting uniqueness.

8. The reduced-cost optimality test

Solution to exercise 8

For j∉Sj\notin S, set c‾j=cj−cS⊺AS−1Aj\bar c_j=c_j-c_S^\intercal A_S^{-1}A_j. For any feasible zz, zS=xS−AS−1AS‾zS‾,c⊺z−c⊺x=∑j∉Sc‾jzj≥0. z_S=x_S-A_S^{-1}A_{\bar S}z_{\bar S}, \qquad c^\intercal z-c^\intercal x =\sum_{j\notin S}\bar c_j z_j\ge 0. The proof also holds at a degenerate BFS. Degeneracy can instead cause a zero-length pivot when an entering index has negative reduced cost. The PDF asks why the sufficient optimality proof fails, but that premise is false.

9. Vertices and reduced costs

Minimize x1−x2x_1-x_2 subject to x1+2x2≤4x_1+2x_2\le 4, 2x1+x2≤52x_1+x_2\le 5, and x1,x2≥0x_1,x_2\ge 0.

10. Feasible directions at a BFS

Solution to exercise 10

11. An initial basis for the auxiliary LP

Minimize 𝟏⊺z\mathbf 1^\intercal z subject to Ax+Dz=bAx+Dz=b and x,z≥0x,z\ge 0, where DD is diagonal with Dii=1D_{ii}=1 for bi≥0b_i\ge 0 and Dii=−1D_{ii}=-1 otherwise.

  1. What conditions must a BFS satisfy?
  2. Give a BFS for the auxiliary problem.
  3. If the original LP with Ax=bAx=b and x≥0x\ge 0 is feasible, what is the optimal value of the auxiliary problem?
Solution to exercise 11

A BFS satisfies the constraints and has an invertible set of mm columns of [AD][A\ D] with all other variables zero. Use the columns of DD as the basis, with x=0x=0 and z=D−1b=|b|z=D^{-1}b=|b| entrywise. When x̂\widehat x is feasible for the original LP, (x̂,0)(\widehat x,0) is feasible for the auxiliary LP and has objective zero. The objective is always nonnegative, so zero is optimal.
